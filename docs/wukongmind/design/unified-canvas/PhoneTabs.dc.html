Harbour towns bet on night ferries to ease the summer rush

By [AUTHOR] · 6 min read

The ferry office in Saltby opened its night timetable in May, and by the second weekend the 23:40 sailing ran full. Harbour staff say the late boats take pressure off the afternoon queue, when day visitors and freight share the same ramp.

Northwick followed in June with two crossings after ten. The council's transport lead called it a test that can be stopped in a week if the numbers fall, but the first month brought more riders than the morning boat.

Three more towns plan to start next summer. Their harbours need lighting along the quay and a second ticket window, which the regional fund has agreed to pay for if the trial towns keep their numbers through August.

Residents near the slipways have asked for quieter engines after midnight. The operator says the newer boats on the Saltby route already meet the limit, and the older pair will move to daytime runs.

For now the late timetable runs Thursday to Sunday. Tickets are the same price as daytime crossings, and bikes travel free on every night sailing until the end of the season.
