Privacy Policy

Forumind for iPhone and iPad Effective date: November 7, 2026

Forumind is a free, open-source app that helps you read Discourse forums, WordPress sites and Hacker News with an AI assistant. This policy explains, in plain words, what the app does with your information. The short version: the developer collects nothing. There are no Forumind servers, accounts, analytics, advertising, or tracking.

What the app handles, and where it goes

Site pages and your login

The app has a built-in web browser. When you open a forum or site, the page loads directly from that site, as it would in Safari. If you log in, the site's login cookies are stored on your device by iOS's web view and are sent only to that site, never to another site.

When you ask for a summary, a chat answer, or an Ask the forum search, the app reads posts and comments directly from that site (including Discourse topic and search endpoints, and WordPress /wp-json/wp/v2/ post, comment, and category endpoints), using your login on sites that support it so it can read what you can read.

Hacker News is the exception: its stories, comments and searches are read from Algolia's public Hacker News API (hn.algolia.com), without any login. Algolia receives those requests, like any website you visit.

Your AI provider

To summarize or answer, the app sends the relevant forum posts and your question to the AI service you choose:

  • Apple Intelligence (where available): requests are processed by Apple's on-device model, or by Apple's Private Cloud Compute, which Apple designed so that your data is used only to fulfill your request and isn't stored or made accessible to Apple. See Apple's privacy information on Apple Intelligence.
  • A provider you connect (for example OpenAI, Anthropic, Google Gemini, Google Vertex AI, OpenRouter, Groq, xAI, DeepSeek, or NVIDIA NIM): requests go straight from your device to that provider using your own API key, and that provider's privacy policy applies to what you send it. Before the first request to each such provider, the app asks for your consent on a page that names the provider, says what is sent, and links to its terms and privacy policy. If you don't allow it, nothing is sent to that provider. You can see where each feature sends data, and withdraw or give consent, in Settings › Data & privacy.
  • A model on your own computer (Ollama or LM Studio): requests go to that computer over your local network and nowhere else.

Nothing is sent to an AI service until you start a summary, chat, search, or voice session, make a daily digest or its podcast (or turn on the daily digest or podcast, which are made when you open the app), use read aloud with Google's voices, or turn on automatic summary refresh for a watched topic.

Live voice (optional)

Voice lets you talk to the app about the forum page you're reading. It is off until you turn it on or tap the microphone in the browser bar, and the app asks for your consent, naming Google, before the first session with each provider.

  • While a session runs, your voice (the microphone audio) and the page content you ask about (post text, titles, and search results) go straight from your device to Google (Gemini Live, through Google AI Studio or Google Vertex AI) with your own API key. There is no Forumind server in between.
  • On the free tier of Google AI Studio, Google's terms allow it to use what you send to improve its products, and people may review it. Don't say or open anything you want to keep private. Paid AI Studio use and Vertex AI follow Google's paid-service terms. See the Gemini API Additional Terms of Service and the Google Cloud Service Specific Terms.
  • The app doesn't record or keep the audio. It keeps only the minutes you were connected, on this device, for the time limits in Settings › Voice. You can withdraw consent there.
  • The microphone is used only during a session (and, as before, when a forum page asks to record a video). iOS shows the microphone indicator while it is on, including when the screen is locked during a session.

Podcast and read aloud (optional)

The Today page can turn your digest into a short spoken podcast, and summaries and digest cards can be read aloud.

  • The podcast's script is written by your AI provider, like the digest itself, from the digest's cards and the topics they're about.
  • Reading it out uses either this device's voices, which are free, work offline and send nothing, or Google's voices (Gemini text-to-speech, through Google AI Studio or Google Vertex AI) when you have a Google key. With Google's voices, the text to be spoken (the script, a summary or a card) goes straight from your device to Google with your own API key. No audio is recorded. The app asks for your consent, naming Google, before it first sends text this way. You can withdraw that consent in Settings › Daily digest, separately from the voice consent.
  • The same free-tier terms apply as for voice: on the free tier of Google AI Studio, Google may use what you send to improve its products, and people may review it.
  • The podcast is made only while the app is open. Making it every day is off until you turn it on.
  • The audio is kept in the app's cache on your device, with its digest, for at most 7 days. It is deleted with its digest. iOS may also clear it.

What's stored on your device

  • Your summaries, chats, Ask the forum runs, watched topics, forums, and settings are stored in the app's private storage on your device.
  • Daily digests (the last 7), the forum data fetched for them, and their podcast audio stay on your device; they aren't synced. Delete them in Settings › Daily digest.
  • Chats and activity you haven't marked Keep are cleared after a day; summaries are kept up to a limit. You can delete data per forum, or everything, in Settings › Data & privacy.
  • API keys are stored in the iOS Keychain, not in the app's data file. If Sync API keys is on (the default), they are stored in iCloud Keychain so your other devices can use them. Apple end-to-end encrypts iCloud Keychain. Keys never go into the CloudKit database.

Spotlight, Siri and translation

  • Spotlight. The titles of topics you've read, their forum's name and the start of their saved summary are added to Spotlight on your device, so you can search for them there. This index stays on your device: it is never public, and the app doesn't send it anywhere. Deleting a summary or a forum, or clearing your data, removes its entries. Turning off Spotlight in Settings › Data & privacy deletes the app's whole index.
  • Siri suggestions. When you open a topic, the app tells iOS about it, so Siri can suggest it again on this device. This isn't used for search, Handoff or public indexing. Turn it off with Siri Suggestions in Settings › Data & privacy, which also deletes what was shared.
  • Shortcuts and Siri. The app's actions (Summarize Link, Ask a Forum, and others) run in the app on your device. Summarize Link reads the clipboard only when you run it without a link. A question you ask goes to your AI provider, as it would if you typed it in the app.
  • Translation (iOS 18 or later) uses Apple's Translation framework on your device. The text isn't sent to the developer or to your AI provider. iOS may download a language the first time you use it.

iCloud sync

When your device is signed in to iCloud, the app syncs your forums, summaries, chats, Ask the forum runs, watched topics, and settings between your devices through Apple CloudKit, in the app's private database in your own iCloud account. The content (forum names and addresses, topic titles, summaries, chats, answers, and settings) is stored in CloudKit's end-to-end encrypted fields, with keys held by your devices; Apple and the developer can't read it. Like any CloudKit data, each record also has unencrypted metadata that CloudKit needs to work, such as its type, an identifier, and when it changed.

The developer has no access to your iCloud account or to your private database: CloudKit gives the developer no way to read a user's private data. The data counts toward your iCloud storage. Turn sync off in Settings › iCloud Sync, and use Delete iCloud data there to remove the app's data from iCloud on all your devices (data on each device is kept).

CloudKit tells the app about changes from your other devices with silent push notifications from Apple, which carry no content and show nothing on screen.

Notifications

If you watch a topic and allow notifications, the app checks that topic on the forum (including in the background, when iOS allows) and shows a local notification about new replies. If you turn on the daily digest, the app reads your pinned forums' topic lists and your watched topics on the forums in the background while the device is charging and online, without any AI request, and can show a local notification that the digest is ready to make. These notifications are created on your device; they don't go through a push notification service.

Ad and tracker blocking

The browser can block ads and trackers using filter lists that are bundled with the app (EasyList and EasyPrivacy). Blocking is off by default, out of respect for forum owners who rely on ads; turn it on, or allow a site, in Settings › Browser (or from the shield in the address bar). Blocking happens on your device; the app doesn't send your browsing to any list provider.

Moderation

You can block forum users and filter words (Settings › Data & privacy); these choices are stored with your settings (and synced with them). Once a day the app downloads the developer's public list of removed content (https://github.com/kccarlos/forumind/tree/main/moderation) from GitHub; this is a plain request for a public file and sends nothing about you.

If you report content or block a user (⋯ › Report or block), your email app opens with a report to the developer that includes the forum, topic or post link, the reason, and any details you add. It's sent only if you send it, from your own email account, and is used only to review the report.

Sharing to the app

When you share a page from Safari, Chrome, or another app to Forumind, the share extension passes the page's address and title to the app on your device. Nothing else is read from the sharing app.

What the developer collects

Nothing, apart from reports you choose to email (see Moderation). The app has no analytics, crash-reporting SDK, advertising, or tracking, and no third-party code. The developer does not operate servers that receive your data. The app does not track you across apps or websites.

If you choose to share crash reports and usage data with app developers in iOS Settings, Apple may provide the developer with anonymous crash reports through App Store Connect; that is controlled by you in iOS and by Apple's privacy policy.

Children

The app is not directed at children. It includes a web browser that can open any website.

Open source

The app's complete source code is public at https://github.com/kccarlos/forumind, so anyone can check what it does.
